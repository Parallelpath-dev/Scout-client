Under the hood

Method

How this quarterly roll-up is bounded
  1. The reporting period is Q3 2026 — 1 July to 30 September, 92 days, complete. Every headline number, market row and rate on this page describes that window and nothing else. No October data is present anywhere in the pipeline.
  2. The baseline window is wider than the reporting window, and deliberately so. A post is scored against every comparable post on record, including from 20 May to 30 June. Discarding six weeks of peer data to make the window tidy would weaken every score on the page and buy nothing.
  3. Month detail survives in exactly one place. The syndication trajectory — five drops in July, two in August, none in September — only exists as a sequence. Reporting it as "seven drops in Q3" would delete the finding. Everywhere else, the quarter is the unit.
  4. There is no quarter-over-quarter comparison because there is no prior quarter. The record opens 20 May, so a "Q2" would be 42 days against 92. An honest comparison arrives with Q4.
Why the statistics are built this way
  1. Engagement rate, not raw counts. Otherwise Seattle beats Boston by construction and the comparison measures audience size, not content.
  2. Log space. Engagement is log-normal with a long right tail; one breakout reel pulls a raw mean above most of the data.
  3. Median and MAD (×1.4826), never mean and standard deviation. Using the mean to find outliers is circular — the outliers move the mean.
  4. Baselines per-market and per-format. A carousel in Tempe is not comparable to a reel in Seattle.
  5. Leave-one-out. A post never influences the bar it is judged against.
  6. Follower counts interpolated across three snapshots (20 Aug, 25 Sep, 2 Oct), so a September post is measured against September's audience rather than July's.
  7. Pinned posts excluded before any statistic — they accumulate engagement indefinitely at the top of a profile.
  8. An explicit "no baseline" state. Under eight comparable posts the system returns nothing rather than guessing. posts in the quarter sit in that state.
  9. The scoring method has not changed since the first run. Same log space, same median/MAD, same leave-one-out, same thresholds. What moves on this page is the content.
  10. Confounds not controlled for. Time of day, day of week, boosted posts, re-shares by high-reach accounts, seasonality. All fixable with account access; none from outside.

Post-level aggregates only. No commenter identities, captions, profile photos or tagged-user records were retained at any point in the pipeline.
