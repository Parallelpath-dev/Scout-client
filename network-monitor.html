Where a human takes over

The review queue

Flags, not verdicts. The machine cannot see what a video was about, whether a gym was shut for a comp, or whether a post did an operational job engagement will never capture. A strategist reads these, tags the theme, and decides what reaches the market teams. Links go to the live posts.

The ceiling

What we can see, and what we can't

Two runs in, the limits are the same ones — and five weeks of extra data has made a couple of them sharper, not softer.

Available from outside

  • Likes and comment counts, every post, every market
  • Reel play counts — a genuine reach denominator
  • Format, timestamp to the minute, pinned status
  • Follower counts, now at two points in time
  • Caption text and hashtags

Invisible without access

  • Reach on carousels and stills — and that blind spot just grew, because those formats went from 37% of output to 49%
  • Saves and shares — the signals that predict intent
  • Follower vs. non-follower split — new climbers, or the same regulars?
  • Profile taps and link clicks — the bridge from post to day pass
  • Paid amplification — a boosted post silently corrupts an organic baseline
  • Whether any of this filled a gym

What each unlock buys

Meta Business Suite → every format gets a denominator

This mattered more this month than last. The network shifted output toward the two formats we cannot measure reach on, so the share of content we can only judge by likes went up. We can tell you engagement rate rose on carousels; we cannot tell you whether that is better content or simply fewer people seeing it.

GA4 and site access → engagement becomes traffic

Ties Instagram sessions to day-pass pages, membership enquiries and class bookings. The schedule carousel that looks flat on likes may be the best traffic driver on the account, and nothing here can tell you that.

Membership data → traffic becomes revenue

Closes the loop from post to trial to joined member, per market. It is also the only version of this that can stop the system recommending a gym delete the posts that keep the gym running.

Per-market dashboards → the same engine, nine views

Each market sees its own baselines, its own flags, and the themes working elsewhere in the network that it has not tried — without a leaderboard that turns nine teams against each other. The operating team keeps this aggregate view.

Under the hood

Method

What changed between run 1 and run 2
  1. The scoring method deliberately did not change. Switching to a rolling primary score now would confound "the content changed" with "the method changed" in every comparison on this page. Method changes wait for a run where nothing else moves.
  2. Follower counts are no longer one snapshot. We hold Aug 20 and Sep 25 and interpolate between them, so a September post is measured against September's audience. Growth ranged from +0.9% to +3.3% over five weeks.
  3. The baseline the August posts are judged against got bigger, because five weeks of new posts joined the peer pool. That legitimately moves some run-1 flags, and the count is reported above rather than quietly absorbed.
  4. The rolling 90-day baseline runs for the first time, reported alongside the primary score rather than replacing it.
Why the statistics are built this way
  1. Engagement rate, not raw counts. Otherwise Seattle beats Boston by construction and the comparison measures audience size, not content.
  2. Log space. Engagement is log-normal with a long right tail; one breakout reel pulls a raw mean above most of the data.
  3. Median and MAD (×1.4826), never mean and standard deviation. Using the mean to find outliers is circular — the outliers move the mean.
  4. Baselines per-market and per-format. A carousel in Tempe is not comparable to a reel in Seattle.
  5. Leave-one-out. A post never influences the bar it is judged against.
  6. Pinned posts excluded before any statistic is computed — they accumulate engagement indefinitely at the top of a profile.
  7. An explicit "no baseline" state. Under eight comparable posts the system returns nothing. Two markets have no period-B reel efficiency for exactly this reason. Refusing to answer is a feature.
  8. Confounds not controlled for. Time of day, day of week, boosted posts, re-shares by high-reach accounts, seasonality, and a three-fold spread in posting cadence. All fixable with account access; none fixable from outside.

Post-level aggregates only. No commenter identities, captions, profile photos or tagged-user records were retained at any point in the pipeline.
